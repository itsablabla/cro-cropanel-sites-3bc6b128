Said before buying — these are conversion problems

Unexpected fees at checkout
The user is enticed with a low advertised price. After investing time and effort, they discover unexpected fees and charges when they reach the checkout.
— deceptive.design
Prospects are frustrated when the final price is higher than advertised due to hidden charges revealed late in the buying process, leading to abandonment or dissatisfaction.
Confusing pricing structures
frequent price changes may confuse or upset customers.
— netsuite.com
Inconsistent or frequently changing prices, complex bundles, and unclear loyalty programs create uncertainty for potential buyers, making comparison difficult.
Unclear return and refund terms
Most customers want to know how they can return or exchange an item if they're unhappy with it, which is why you need to display your Refund Policy somewhere obvious.
— privacypolicies.com
Prospects feel risky or confused when the conditions, timelines, or options for returns and refunds are not clearly communicated upfront, impacting their willingness to buy.
Misunderstanding insurance coverage/copayment
They messed my insurance copayment information; they charged me co-payment where my insurance cover full.
— embedsocial.com
Prospects may proceed with a purchase expecting full coverage, only to find out later they were charged a copayment their insurance should have covered, leading to a sense of being misled.
Complex cancellation policies
To receive a refund, customers must be notified at least 4 days before the start of the event. In all other instances, only a store credit is issued.
— help.getoccasion.com
Potential customers are deterred or confused by intricate rules and deadlines for cancelling services or events, especially regarding refunds versus store credit, which increases perceived risk.
